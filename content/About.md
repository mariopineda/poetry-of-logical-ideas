# About The Poetry of Logical Ideas

## What?

**The Poetry of Logical Ideas** is a collection of mathematics resources for my students.

It contains course information, frequently asked questions, practice questions, notes, examples, and other materials that may be useful during the semester.

It is not intended to be a textbook, and it is not meant to replace what happens in class. Think of it instead as a growing mathematical reference: a place where ideas, questions, examples, and course resources can accumulate and connect over time.

## Why?

Mathematics is often presented as a sequence of procedures:

learn the rule, copy the example, do the exercise.

But mathematics is much more interesting than that.

It is a way of reasoning. It is a language for describing patterns and relationships. It can be practical, abstract, surprising, frustrating, elegant, and occasionally beautiful.

This site exists partly to make the practical side of the course easier to navigate, but also to leave room for that larger view of mathematics.

## Who?

This site is maintained by your math teacher and is primarily intended for students in my classes.

Some pages deal directly with how the class operates. Others contain mathematical material that may be useful for review, practice, or simply exploring an idea a little further.

The collection will change as the semester progresses.

## How?

The site is deliberately organized more like a web than a book.

You do not necessarily have to begin at page one and move forward in order. Follow links. Search for a topic. Move backward when you need a prerequisite idea and forward when something catches your interest.

If you are looking for something specific, the search box is often the fastest place to begin.

For questions about how the course works, start with the **[[Frequently Asked Questions|Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)]]**.

## Why “The Poetry of Logical Ideas”?

Mathematics is built on logic, but good mathematics is not merely mechanical.

A particularly elegant solution can compress a complicated argument into a few lines. A surprising connection can suddenly make two apparently unrelated ideas fit together. A good proof or mathematical explanation can have rhythm, economy, structure, and even beauty.

There is something almost poetic about that.

**The Poetry of Logical Ideas** is my name for that side of mathematics: the point where careful reasoning becomes something more than calculation.

## When?

Now.

Like the mathematics it contains, this site is a work in progress.
