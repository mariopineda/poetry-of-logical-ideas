Express the following rational expressions in simplest form, stating the non-permissible values as restrictions.

1.

$$
\displaystyle\frac{9c^2}{27c}
$$
2.

$$
\displaystyle\frac{35n^5}{15n^{10}}
$$
3.

$$
\displaystyle\frac{(q-6)(q+2)}{(q+5)(q+2)}
$$
4.

$$
\displaystyle\frac{(r-4)(r+9)}{r(r+9)(r-12)}
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### 1.
>
> The denominator cannot equal zero, so
>
> $$
> c\ne0.
> $$
>
> Simplify:
>
> $$
> \frac{9c^2}{27c}
> =\frac{c}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{c}{3}},\qquad \boxed{c\ne0}.
> $$
>
> ### 2.
>
> The denominator cannot equal zero, so
>
> $$
> n\ne0.
> $$
>
> Simplify the numerical coefficients and subtract exponents:
>
> $$
> \frac{35n^5}{15n^{10}}
> =\frac{7}{3}n^{-5}
> =\frac{7}{3n^5}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{7}{3n^5}},\qquad \boxed{n\ne0}.
> $$
>
> ### 3.
>
> From the original denominator,
>
> $$
> (q+5)(q+2)\ne0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> q\ne-5,\qquad q\ne-2.
> $$
>
> Cancel the common factor:
>
> $$
> \frac{(q-6)(q+2)}{(q+5)(q+2)}
> =\frac{q-6}{q+5}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{q-6}{q+5}},\qquad \boxed{q\ne-5,-2}.
> $$
>
> ### 4.
>
> From the original denominator,
>
> $$
> r(r+9)(r-12)\ne0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> r\ne0,\qquad r\ne-9,\qquad r\ne12.
> $$
>
> Cancel the common factor:
>
> $$
> \frac{(r-4)(r+9)}{r(r+9)(r-12)}
> =\frac{r-4}{r(r-12)}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{r-4}{r(r-12)}},\qquad
> \boxed{r\ne-9,0,12}.
> $$
