For $f(x)=\dfrac{x-4}{x^2-x-12}$, find all excluded $x$-values, identify any vertical asymptote, and determine the coordinate of the removable discontinuity. Give the horizontal asymptote.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Factor the denominator exactly:
>
> $$x^2-x-12=(x-4)(x+3).$$
>
> Before cancelling, the original function excludes **both** $x=4$ and $x=-3$. For permitted $x$ it becomes
>
> $$f(x)=\frac{1}{x+3}.$$
>
> At $x=4$ the cancelled factor creates a removable hole; its y-coordinate is the limiting value $1/(4+3)=1/7$. Thus the hole is $\boxed{(4,1/7)}$. The remaining denominator zero gives the vertical asymptote $\boxed{x=-3}$; since the simplified function tends to zero for $|x|\to\infty$, the horizontal asymptote is $\boxed{y=0}$. Domain: $\boxed{\mathbb R\setminus\{-3,4\}}$.
