Simplify each quotient and state all non-permissible values as restrictions on the variable.

### A.

$$
\frac{10y^3}{7}\div\frac{15y}{14}
$$

### B.

$$
\frac{y-5}{y+2}\div\frac{3y-15}{y-4}
$$

### C.

$$
\frac{y^2-36}{y-1}\div\frac{y+6}{y+3}
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A.
>
> The divisor cannot equal zero:
>
> $$
> \frac{15y}{14}\ne0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> y\ne0.
> $$
>
> Multiply by the reciprocal:
>
> $$
> \frac{10y^3}{7}\div\frac{15y}{14}
> =
> \frac{10y^3}{7}\cdot\frac{14}{15y}
> =
> \frac{4y^2}{3}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{4y^2}{3}},\qquad \boxed{y\ne0}.
> $$
>
> ### B.
>
> The original denominators give
>
> $$
> y\ne-2,\qquad y\ne4.
> $$
>
> The divisor must also be nonzero:
>
> $$
> 3y-15=3(y-5)\ne0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> y\ne5.
> $$
>
> Factor and multiply by the reciprocal:
>
> $$
> \frac{y-5}{y+2}\div\frac{3(y-5)}{y-4}
> =
> \frac{y-5}{y+2}\cdot\frac{y-4}{3(y-5)}
> =
> \frac{y-4}{3(y+2)}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{y-4}{3(y+2)}},
> \qquad
> \boxed{y\ne-2,4,5}.
> $$
>
> ### C.
>
> The original denominators give
>
> $$
> y\ne1,\qquad y\ne-3.
> $$
>
> The divisor must also be nonzero:
>
> $$
> y+6\ne0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> y\ne-6.
> $$
>
> Factor the difference of squares and multiply by the reciprocal:
>
> $$
> \frac{(y-6)(y+6)}{y-1}
> \div
> \frac{y+6}{y+3}
> =
> \frac{(y-6)(y+6)}{y-1}
> \cdot
> \frac{y+3}{y+6}
> =
> \frac{(y-6)(y+3)}{y-1}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{(y-6)(y+3)}{y-1}},
> \qquad
> \boxed{y\ne-6,-3,1}.
> $$
