Consider

$$
r(x)=\frac{6x+9}{3x-12}.
$$

1. Rewrite $r(x)$ in the form

$$
A+\frac{B}{x-4},
$$

where $A$ and $B$ are constants.
2. State the domain restriction.
3. Use the rewritten form to state the vertical and horizontal asymptotes.
4. Determine the $x$-intercept.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> First factor out $3$:
>
> $$
> r(x)=\frac{3(2x+3)}{3(x-4)}=\frac{2x+3}{x-4}.
> $$
>
> Rewrite the numerator:
>
> $$
> 2x+3=2(x-4)+11.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> r(x)
> &=\frac{2(x-4)+11}{x-4}\\
> &=2+\frac{11}{x-4}.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Hence,
>
> $$
> \boxed{A=2,\qquad B=11}.
> $$
>
> The original denominator gives
>
> $$
> \boxed{x\ne4}.
> $$
>
> From
>
> $$
> r(x)=2+\frac{11}{x-4},
> $$
>
> the vertical asymptote is
>
> $$
> \boxed{x=4}
> $$
>
> and the horizontal asymptote is
>
> $$
> \boxed{y=2}.
> $$
>
> For the $x$-intercept, set the numerator of the original expression equal to zero:
>
> $$
> 6x+9=0.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> x=-\frac32,
> $$
>
> so the $x$-intercept is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\left(-\frac32,0\right)}.
> $$
