Determine all **non-permissible real values** in the rational expression

$$\frac{(x-2)(x+4)}{(x+4)(x-7)}\div\frac{x-2}{(x-7)(x+5)}.$$

Explain why division creates an additional restriction, and simplify the expression for permissible values.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The first denominator excludes $x=-4,7$. The divisor's denominator excludes $x=7,-5$. Because division by zero is not defined, the **divisor itself** must also be nonzero, so $x=2$ is excluded.
>
> Thus the complete set is $\boxed{\{-5,-4,2,7\}}$.
>
> For all permissible values, multiply by the reciprocal:
>
> $$\frac{(x-2)(x+4)}{(x+4)(x-7)}\cdot\frac{(x-7)(x+5)}{x-2}=\boxed{x+5}.$$
>
> The simplified result does not eliminate the four exclusions.
