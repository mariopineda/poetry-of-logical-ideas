## Question

Consider the functions

$$
A(x)=\frac{(x-2)(x+1)}{(x-2)(x+3)}
$$

and

$$
B(x)=\frac{x+1}{x+3}.
$$

1. Simplify $A(x)$ and state its domain restrictions.
2. State the domain restriction for $B(x)$.
3. Are $A$ and $B$ the same function? Justify your answer precisely.
4. Describe how the graph of $A$ differs from the graph of $B$.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> For $A$, the original denominator gives
>
> $$
> x\ne2,-3.
> $$
>
> Cancelling the common factor gives
>
> $$
> A(x)=\frac{x+1}{x+3},\qquad x\ne2,-3.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{A(x)=\frac{x+1}{x+3}},\qquad \boxed{x\ne2,-3}.
> $$
>
> For $B$,
>
> $$
> x+3\ne0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \boxed{x\ne-3}.
> $$
>
> The formulas agree wherever both functions are defined, but the domains are different. In particular,
>
> $$
> B(2)=\frac{3}{5},
> $$
>
> while $A(2)$ is undefined.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{A\text{ and }B\text{ are not the same function}}.
> $$
>
> The graph of $A$ is the graph of $B$ with the point corresponding to $x=2$ removed. Since
>
> $$
> B(2)=\frac35,
> $$
>
> $A$ has a hole at
>
> $$
> \boxed{\left(2,\frac35\right)}.
> $$
