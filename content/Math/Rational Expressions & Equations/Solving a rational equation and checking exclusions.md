A student is solving the rational equation

$$\frac1{x-1}+\frac2{x+2}=1.$$

Identify the original excluded values, then **derive the equivalent quadratic equation** by clearing denominators. Finally, solve and check that no inadmissible root is included.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The denominators require $x\ne1,-2$. Multiply each side by $(x-1)(x+2)$:
>
> $$x+2+2(x-1)=(x-1)(x+2).$$
>
> Therefore $3x=x^2+x-2$, hence the equivalent quadratic equation is
>
> $$\boxed{x^2-2x-2=0}.$$
>
> By the quadratic formula, $x=\dfrac{2\pm\sqrt{4+8}}2=\boxed{1\pm\sqrt3}$. Neither root equals $1$ or $-2$; both satisfy the original equation.
