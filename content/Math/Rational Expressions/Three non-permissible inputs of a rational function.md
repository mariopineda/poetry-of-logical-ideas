Four different rational expressions are listed below. For **each** expression, identify all non-permissible values and explain how the denominator determines them. Which expression has **exactly three distinct** non-permissible values?

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_1(x)&=\frac{3(x+4)}{(x-1)(x+2)}\\[4pt]
R_2(x)&=\frac{x+5}{(x-2)(x+1)(x-4)}\\[4pt]
R_3(x)&=\frac{x-3}{(x+3)^2}\\[4pt]
R_4(x)&=\frac{x+2}{x(x-5)(x^2-9)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Set each denominator factor equal to zero. Distinct roots—not repeated factors—determine the non-permissible values.
>
> - $R_1$: $\boxed{x=1,-2}$ (two values).
> - $R_2$: $\boxed{x=2,-1,4}$ (three values).
> - $R_3$: $\boxed{x=-3}$ (one value; a repeated factor does not count twice).
> - $R_4$: $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$, giving $\boxed{x=0,5,3,-3}$ (four values).
>
> Therefore $\boxed{R_2}$ is the expression with exactly three distinct non-permissible values. These restrictions come from the **original** expressions even if factors subsequently cancel.
