A neighbourhood science society has 14 members eligible for three different positions: chair, secretary and treasurer. No member may hold more than one position. How many assignments of these roles are possible? Show why combinations alone are inappropriate.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The positions are distinct: assigning the same three people to different roles gives a different committee. There are $14$ choices for chair, $13$ for secretary, and $12$ for treasurer.
>
> $$14\times13\times12={}_{14}P_3=\boxed{2184}.$$
>
> A combination would count the selection of the three people but not their allocation to the named offices.
