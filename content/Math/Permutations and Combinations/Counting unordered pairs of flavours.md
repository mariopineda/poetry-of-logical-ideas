A tea shop offers **16 distinct flavour extracts** and makes blended drinks by choosing **two different extracts** for each new recipe.

How many distinct two-extract combinations are possible if the order of mixing does not change the drink? Show an appropriate counting calculation.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The two chosen flavours must be different, and the order of naming them does not create a new mixture. Therefore count unordered pairs:
>
> $$\binom{16}{2}=\frac{16\cdot15}{2}=\boxed{120}.$$
>
> The division by $2$ accounts for counting each pair twice if the flavours had first been ordered.
