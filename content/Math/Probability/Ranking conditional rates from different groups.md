Three museum branches surveyed visitors who purchased a printed exhibition guide.

| Museum branch | Visitors buying a guide | Visitors surveyed |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 10 | 80 |
| B | 15 | 100 |
| C | 9 | 45 |

Rank the museum branches from **lowest to highest** observed probability of purchasing a guide. Show the probability calculation for each district and explain why comparing only the number of guide purchasers would be misleading.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Convert each sample count to a probability using its own branch denominator:
>
> $$P(A)=\frac{10}{80}=0.125,\qquad P(B)=\frac{15}{100}=0.15,\qquad P(C)=\frac9{45}=0.20.$$
>
> Therefore the ascending order is
>
> $$\boxed{A,\ B,\ C}.$$
>
> The absolute numbers of guide buyers cannot be compared directly because the sample sizes differ. Relative frequencies must be used instead.
