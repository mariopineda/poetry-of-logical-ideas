A transport survey recorded $100$ commuters, classified by whether they travel by bicycle ($B$) or walk to work ($W$). The Venn diagram shows how many commuters fall into each category.

![[diploma-batch07-2013-mc16-commuter-venn.svg]]

Determine the **odds in favour** of selecting a bicycle commuter at random. Give the odds as a ratio of whole numbers in lowest terms, explaining how the Venn diagram supplies both parts of the ratio.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Inside the bicycle circle are $21$ commuters who cycle only and $17$ who both cycle and walk. Thus
>
> $$n(B)=21+17=38.$$
>
> The other $20+42=62$ do not belong to $B$. Odds in favour compare favourable outcomes with unfavourable outcomes (not with the entire sample):
>
> $$38:62=\boxed{19:31}.$$
>
> The four Venn regions total $21+17+20+42=100$, confirming that every commuter has been counted once.
