Among **120** randomly surveyed residents, **36** have both a transit pass and a bicycle. A resident is selected at random.

Express the **odds in favour** of selecting someone who has both, in simplest ratio form. Also state the corresponding probability and explain the distinction.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> There are 36 favourable and $120-36=84$ unfavourable residents. Odds in favour are
>
> $$36:84=\boxed{3:7}.$$
>
> The **probability**, by contrast, is favourable divided by **all** outcomes:
>
> $$P=\frac{36}{120}=\boxed{\frac3{10}}.$$
>
> Odds use favourable-to-unfavourable counts, not favourable-to-total counts.
