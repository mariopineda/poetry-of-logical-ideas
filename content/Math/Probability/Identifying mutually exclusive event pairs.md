Consider three single-trial experiments:

- **I:** Toss one coin. Event $X$ is obtaining heads and event $Y$ is obtaining tails.
- **II:** Select one integer uniformly from $1$ through $12$. Event $X$ is selecting a multiple of $2$ and event $Y$ is selecting a multiple of $3$.
- **III:** Draw one single-colour admission ticket from a container. Event $X$ is obtaining a green ticket and event $Y$ is obtaining a gold ticket.

Which experiments have **mutually exclusive** event pairs? Use event intersections to justify your answer.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> **Experiment I:** A single coin toss cannot show both heads and tails, so the events are disjoint.
>
> **Experiment II:** Among the integers $1$ to $12$, multiples of $2$ and multiples of $3$ overlap at $6$ and $12$, so $X\cap Y=\{6,12\}\ne\varnothing$. The events are not mutually exclusive.
>
> **Experiment III:** A single-colour ticket cannot be simultaneously green and gold, so the events are disjoint.
>
> Thus the mutually exclusive pairs are from $\boxed{\text{I and III only}}$.
