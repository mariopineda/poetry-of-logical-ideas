At a concert, a sound source produces $112$ decibels (dB). Its intensity is $2500$ times the intensity of a second source. Sound level follows

$$
L=10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right).
$$

Determine the second sound source's level to the nearest decibel. Explain why the difference is found by taking a logarithm of the intensity ratio.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> Subtracting the two sound-level equations eliminates the reference intensity:
>
> $$
> L_1-L_2=10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{I_1}{I_2}\right)
> =10\log_{10}(2500)\approx33.9794\text{ dB}.
> $$
>
> Therefore
>
> $$
> L_2=112-33.9794\approx78.0206\text{ dB}
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> \boxed{78\text{ dB}}.
> $$
>
> The ratio becomes a difference because $\log A-\log B=\log(A/B)$.
