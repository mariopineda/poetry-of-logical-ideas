Solve the logarithmic equation and verify that your answer is in the original domain.

$$
\log_2(x-1)-\log_2(x-3)=2
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> The logarithms require both $x-1>0$ and $x-3>0$, so the original domain is $x>3$.
>
> $$
> \log_2\!\left(\frac{x-1}{x-3}\right)=2
> \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> \frac{x-1}{x-3}=4.
> $$
>
> Hence $x-1=4x-12$, so $3x=11$ and
>
> $$
> \boxed{x=\frac{11}{3}\approx3.67}.
> $$
>
> Since $\frac{11}{3}>3$, both logarithm arguments are positive. Substitution gives $\frac{x-1}{x-3}=4$, whose base-$2$ logarithm is $2$.
