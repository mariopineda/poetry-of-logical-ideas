The graph shown is $y=a\log_2(x-h)$, with $a\ne0$.

![[diploma-batch05-2016-mc8-log-graph.svg]]

Determine the **signs** of $a$ and $h$. Use both the direction of the curve and the position of its vertical asymptote in your explanation.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> For a function $\log_2(x-h)$ the vertical asymptote is $x=h$. On the graph the asymptote is to the **right** of the $y$-axis, at $x=4$, so $h=4>0$.
>
> Because base $2>1$, $\log_2(x-h)$ increases from left to right. The shown graph decreases, requiring a negative multiplier $a<0$.
>
> $$
> \boxed{a<0,\qquad h>0}.
> $$
