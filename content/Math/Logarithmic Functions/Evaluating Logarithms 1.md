Evaluate by converting to exponential form.

1.

$$
\log_{5} \sqrt{5}
$$
2.

$$
\log_{3} \sqrt{27}
$$
3.

$$
\log_{\frac{1}{2}} \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)
$$
4.

$$
\log_{\frac{1}{2}} 4
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### 1.
>
> Let
>
> $$
> \log_5\sqrt5=x.
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> 5^x=5^{1/2},
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \boxed{x=\frac12}.
> $$
>
> ### 2.
>
> $$
> \sqrt{27}
> =
> \sqrt{3^3}
> =
> 3^{3/2}.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \log_3\sqrt{27}
> =
> \frac32
> }.
> $$
>
> ### 3.
>
> $$
> \log_{\frac12}\left(\frac14\right)=x
> $$
>
> means
>
> $$
> \left(\frac12\right)^x
> =
> \frac14
> =
> \left(\frac12\right)^2.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{x=2}.
> $$
>
> ### 4.
>
> $$
> \log_{\frac12}4=x
> $$
>
> means
>
> $$
> \left(\frac12\right)^x=4=2^2.
> $$
>
> Since
>
> $$
> \left(\frac12\right)^{-2}=4,
> $$
>
> we get
>
> $$
> \boxed{x=-2}.
> $$
