The increasing logarithmic curve $y=\log_b x$ contains the plotted points $Q\left(2,\frac{1}{2}\right)$ and $P\left(k,\frac{3}{2}\right)$.

![[diploma-batch04-2014-mc5-log-points.svg]]

Determine the base $b$ and the unknown $x$-coordinate $k$ **exactly**. Use the relationship between a logarithm and a fractional exponent, not an estimate from the graph.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> A point $(x,y)$ on $y=\log_b x$ satisfies $x=b^y$ with $b>0$ and $b\ne1$.
>
> The point $Q(2,1/2)$ gives
>
> $$2=b^{1/2}=\sqrt b\;\Longrightarrow\;\boxed{b=4}.$$
>
> Then the point $P(k,3/2)$ gives
>
> $$k=b^{3/2}=\left(\sqrt b\right)^3=2^3=\boxed{8}.$$
>
> Both points lie on $y=\log_4 x$, and the graph rises because $4>1$.
