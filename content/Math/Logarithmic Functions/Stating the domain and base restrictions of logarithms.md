A logarithmic model is defined by $f(x)=\log_b(3x-12)$, where $b$ is an unknown constant.

State **all** necessary restrictions on the base $b$ and the input $x$ for $f$ to have real values. Explain why $\log_b(3x-12)$ does not require its output to be positive.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> A real logarithm requires a positive base different from $1$:
>
> $$\boxed{b>0,\quad b\ne1}.$$
>
> Its argument must be strictly positive:
>
> $$3x-12>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{x>4}.$$
>
> The logarithm itself can be positive, zero, or negative depending on its positive argument and the base. Only the **argument**, not the result, is required to be positive.
