A horticulturist measures the length of a vine grown in controlled conditions.

| Age $t$ (weeks) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Length $L$ (cm) | 2.0 | 3.2 | 4.2 | 4.9 | 5.5 | 6.0 |

Use logarithmic regression to model the data in the form $L=a+b\ln t$. Report $a$ and $b$ to three decimal places, and predict the length after $12$ weeks to the nearest tenth of a centimetre. Show the regression method or the values entered into technology.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Let $u=\ln t$, then fit the straight-line model $L=a+bu$ to the six points $(\ln t,L)$ using logarithmic regression or linear least squares.
>
> The coefficients are approximately
>
> $$a=\boxed{1.834},\qquad b=\boxed{2.249}.$$
>
> Using the unrounded regression values gives
>
> $$L(12)=a+b\ln12\approx\boxed{7.4\text{ cm}}.$$
>
> The logarithmic model is consistent with growth that slows as $t$ increases; it should not be assumed accurate indefinitely outside the measured range.
