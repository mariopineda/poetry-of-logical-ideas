A student simplifies the rational expression

$$\frac{x^2-9}{x^2+3x}$$

using these steps:

1. $\displaystyle\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x(x+3)}$
2. $\displaystyle\frac{x-3}{x}$
3. **The only non-permissible value is $x=0$.**

Identify the first incorrect statement and explain how the correct restrictions are determined. Write the simplified expression together with its complete original domain.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The factorization in Step 1 is correct. The cancellation in Step 2 is valid **provided $x+3\ne0$**, and the original denominator also requires $x\ne0$.
>
> The **first incorrect statement is Step 3**, which forgets the value $x=-3$ excluded by the original denominator $x(x+3)$.
>
> $$\boxed{\frac{x-3}{x},\quad x\ne0,-3}.$$
>
> The simplified expression has a removable hole at $x=-3$; simplifying never reinstates an excluded input.
