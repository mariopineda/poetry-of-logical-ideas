A rational function is $R(x)=\dfrac{3x+2}{x-5}$, with original restriction $x\ne5$.

Construct a **different unsimplified rational expression** that has the same values as $R(x)$ wherever both are defined, but whose denominator is also zero at $x=0$ and $x=-4$. Justify the construction and clearly distinguish the two domains.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> To introduce denominator factors with zeros $0$ and $-4$, multiply numerator and denominator by $x(x+4)$:
>
> $$\boxed{\frac{(3x+2)x(x+4)}{(x-5)x(x+4)}}.$$
>
> The constructed expression excludes $\boxed{x=5,0,-4}$; the original excludes only $x=5$.
>
> For $x\ne5,0,-4$, cancellation reproduces $R(x)$, so the two functions agree **on their common domain** but are not identical as functions with these stated domains.
