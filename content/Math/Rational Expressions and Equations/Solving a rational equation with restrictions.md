Solve the rational equation

$$\frac{2x+1}{x-3}-\frac4{x-3}=5.$$

State the non-permissible value, solve algebraically, and check your answer in the **original** equation.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Because the denominator is $x-3$, the original equation requires $x\ne3$. Combine the left side:
>
> $$\frac{2x+1-4}{x-3}=\frac{2x-3}{x-3}=5.$$
>
> Multiply by $x-3$ (permitted only since $x\ne3$):
>
> $$2x-3=5(x-3)=5x-15,\qquad12=3x,\qquad\boxed{x=4}.$$
>
> Check in the original: $(2(4)+1)/(4-3)-4/(4-3)=9-4=5$. Thus the candidate is admissible and the equation has exactly one solution.
