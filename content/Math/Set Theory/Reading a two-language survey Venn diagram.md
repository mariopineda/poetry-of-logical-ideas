A community centre surveys residents who speak Italian ($I$), Polish ($P$), or both. The survey finds:

- **52** residents speak Polish (including bilingual residents).
- **11** speak both languages.
- **18** speak Italian but not Polish.
- Everyone surveyed speaks at least one of these languages.

Complete the three regions of the blank Venn diagram and find the total number surveyed.

![[diploma-batch08-2016-mc6-blank-two-language-venn.svg]]

Explain why placing 52 in the Polish-only region would be incorrect.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Because the Polish total includes bilingual speakers,
>
> $$P\text{-only}=52-11=\boxed{41}.$$
>
> Place **18** in the Italian-only region, **11** in the overlap, and **41** in the Polish-only region of the diagram. The union includes all three disjoint regions:
>
> $$n(I\cup P)=18+11+41=\boxed{70\text{ residents}}.$$
>
> Placing 52 in the Polish-only region would count the 11 bilingual residents twice when computing the total.
