# Graphing Exponential Functions 1

Sketch each function, label the $y$-intercept, and give its $x$-intercepts, end behaviour, domain, and range.

a) $$
y=8(3)^x
$$
b) $$
y=5(2)^x
$$
c) $$
y=18\left(\frac13\right)^x
$$
d) $$
y=7\left(\frac12\right)^x
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> **Step 1 — Make a small table of values.** Evaluate each equation at $x=-1$, $x=0$, and $x=1$. For example, $8(3)^{-1}=\frac83$, $8(3)^0=8$, and $8(3)^1=24$.
> 
> | Equation | $y(-1)$ | $y(0)$ | $y(1)$ | Trend |
> |---|---:|---:|---:|---|
> | a) $8(3)^x$ | $\frac83$ | $8$ | $24$ | Growth |
> | b) $5(2)^x$ | $\frac52$ | $5$ | $10$ | Growth |
> | c) $18(\frac13)^x$ | $54$ | $18$ | $6$ | Decay |
> | d) $7(\frac12)^x$ | $14$ | $7$ | $\frac72$ | Decay |
> 
> **Step 2 — Sketch.** Plot the three points for each function and draw a smooth curve that approaches, but never touches, the $x$-axis. Label the $y$-intercept $(0,a)$.
> 
> **Step 3 — State the remaining characteristics.** All four have zero $x$-intercepts, domain $x\in\mathbb R$, range $y>0$, and horizontal asymptote $y=0$. For a and b, $y\to0^+$ as $x\to-\infty$ and $y\to\infty$ as $x\to\infty$. For c and d, these directions are reversed.
> 
> **Why this works:** The coefficient $a$ determines the intercept because $b^0=1$. The base $b$ determines whether the function rises ($b>1$) or falls ($0<b<1$).
