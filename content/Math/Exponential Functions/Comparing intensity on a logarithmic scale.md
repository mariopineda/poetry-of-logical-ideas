An industrial vibration scale assigns a reading $M$ to intensity $I$ using

$$
I=I_0\,10^M,
$$

where $I_0$ is a fixed reference intensity. Two measurements have readings $5.1$ and $5.9$.

Determine how many times as intense the **second measurement** is as the first, to the nearest tenth. Explain why the actual value of $I_0$ is unnecessary.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Let $I_1=I_0 10^{5.1}$ and $I_2=I_0 10^{5.9}$. Form the ratio:
>
> $$
> \frac{I_2}{I_1}=\frac{I_0 10^{5.9}}{I_0 10^{5.1}}
> =10^{5.9-5.1}=10^{0.8}.
> $$
>
> Numerically,
>
> $$
> 10^{0.8}\approx6.30957\approx\boxed{6.3}.
> $$
>
> The constant reference intensity $I_0$ cancels; only the **difference** in readings determines the intensity ratio.
