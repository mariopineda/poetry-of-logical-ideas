Suppose $p$ and $q$ are distinct positive real numbers. Solve for $x$ in the equation $\left(\frac pq\right)^{3x+2}=\left(\frac qp\right)^{2x-8}$. Explain why the base restriction permits equating the exponents.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Since $p,q>0$ and $p\ne q$, the common positive base $p/q$ is neither zero nor one. Write the reciprocal on the right as a negative power:
>
> $$\left(\frac pq\right)^{3x+2}=\left(\frac pq\right)^{-2x+8}.$$
>
> The exponential map is one-to-one for this base; hence
>
> $$3x+2=-2x+8\;\Longrightarrow\;5x=6\;\Longrightarrow\;\boxed{x=\frac65}.$$
>
> Substituting $x=6/5$ yields exponent $28/5$ on both sides.
