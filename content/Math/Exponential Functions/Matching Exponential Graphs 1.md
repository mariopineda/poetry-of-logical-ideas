A. $y=3(0.65)^x$

B. $y=2(3)^x$

C. $y=5(0.4)^x$

D. $y=4(1.7)^x$

E. $y=6(1.15)^x$

F. $y=4^x$.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> **Step 1 — Match the $y$-intercepts.** Every equation has the form $y=ab^x$, so its intercept is $a$ (because $b^0=1$). This usually eliminates several graphs immediately.
> 
> **Step 2 — Compare the direction and steepness.** A base below $1$ produces decay; a base above $1$ produces growth. For increasing graphs, a larger base causes steeper growth; for decreasing graphs, a smaller positive base causes faster decay.
> 
> | Equation | Intercept | Type | Matching graph |
> |---|---:|---|---|
> | A. $3(0.65)^x$ | $3$ | Decay | 4 |
> | B. $2(3)^x$ | $2$ | Fast growth | 2 |
> | C. $5(0.4)^x$ | $5$ | Faster decay | 1 |
> | D. $4(1.7)^x$ | $4$ | Growth | 6 |
> | E. $6(1.15)^x$ | $6$ | Slow growth | 3 |
> | F. $4^x$ | $1$ | Very fast growth | 5 |
> 
> **Check:** A point at $x=1$ has height $ab$. For example, equation D has $y(1)=4(1.7)=6.8$. Use this second point if two graphs look similar.
