Two volunteers take part in a supervised classroom study of how caffeine levels decline over time. The volunteers begin with different amounts of caffeine in their systems, and the graphs show the estimated amount remaining, in milligrams, during the eight hours after the initial measurement. Use the graphs to compare both the starting quantities and how rapidly they decrease.

![[EF_T1_Q11.png]]

A. Read the initial amount, contextual domain and range for each.

B. Explain the downward trends.

C. Identify who began with more caffeine and who eliminates it faster proportionally.

D. Estimate both amounts after four hours.

E. Interpret the intercepts in context.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> **Step 1 — Identify what the axes represent.** Time $t$ is measured in hours on the horizontal axis; the amount of caffeine remaining, $C$, is measured in milligrams on the vertical axis.
> 
> **Step 2 — Read the intercepts and trends.** Volunteer A starts at $96$ mg, whereas volunteer B starts at $120$ mg. The graph falls because caffeine is being removed from the body over time. Volunteer B starts with more and has the faster **proportional** decline (its curve falls more sharply relative to its starting value).
> 
> **Step 3 — Read the four-hour values.** At $t=4$, move vertically from the $x$-axis to each curve, then across to the $y$-axis: A is about $32.0$ mg and B about $16.6$ mg.
> 
> **Step 4 — State the domain and range carefully.** For the **eight-hour graph shown**, $0\le t\le8$. Over that displayed interval, A has approximately $10.7\le C\le96$ mg; B approximately $2.3\le C\le120$ mg. A longer-term model instead has $t\ge0$ and $0<C\le C(0)$.
> 
> **Interpretation:** The $y$-intercept represents the initial quantity of caffeine, not the amount absorbed or removed later. The intercepts differ because the volunteers started with different quantities.
