A learner tries to solve $3^{2x+1}=9^{4-x}$ and records:

1. $3^{2x+1}=3^{4-x}$
2. $2x+1=4-x$
3. $3x=3$
4. $x=1$

Identify the **first incorrect step**, explain the exponent-law error, and solve the original equation correctly.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The first error is **Step 1**: $9^{4-x}=(3^2)^{4-x}=3^{8-2x}$, **not** $3^{4-x}$.
>
> The correct equation is $3^{2x+1}=3^{8-2x}$. Equate exponents:
>
> $$2x+1=8-2x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad4x=7\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{x=\tfrac74}.$$
>
> Checking gives both exponents $2(7/4)+1=9/2$ and $2(4-7/4)=9/2$.
