A calibration curve is defined for all real inputs by $f(x)=9(0.6)^x-16$. Determine the $y$-intercept of its inverse function $f^{-1}$ to the nearest hundredth, and explain how the intercepts of a function and its inverse are related.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The $y$-intercept of $f^{-1}$ corresponds to the **$x$-intercept** of $f$ because inverse functions interchange coordinates. Set $f(x)=0$:
>
> $$9(0.6)^x-16=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0.6)^x=\frac{16}{9}.$$
>
> Taking logarithms yields
>
> $$x=\frac{\ln(16/9)}{\ln(0.6)}\approx -1.12634.$$
>
> Because $0<0.6<1$, the exponential function is one-to-one, and the inverse exists over the full function range. Therefore the inverse's $y$-intercept is $\boxed{(0,-1.13)}$.
