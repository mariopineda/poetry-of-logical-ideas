A technician plots an exponential signal $y=3(2^x)$ and a horizontal threshold $y=4$ on the same coordinate plane.

![[batch09-exponential-intersection.svg]]

Using the graph, **estimate** the $x$-coordinate where the signal reaches the threshold. Then calculate this coordinate to the nearest hundredth and explain how the intersection represents the solution to $4=3(2^x)$.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The graph shows one intersection, slightly to the right of $x=0$. The intersection is where the two $y$-values are equal:
>
> $$4=3(2^x)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2^x=\frac43.$$
>
> Take logarithms of both sides:
>
> $$x=\frac{\ln(4/3)}{\ln2}\approx\boxed{0.42}.$$
>
> The exponential is strictly increasing, so it meets the horizontal line exactly once. The graph and the algebra agree.
