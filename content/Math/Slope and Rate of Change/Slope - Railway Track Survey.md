# Slope - Railway Track Survey

A railway surveyor records three points along a proposed railway track. All coordinates are measured in metres. Vertical coordinates represent elevations relative to a reference level.

$$A(-150,-4),\quad B(-50,-1),\quad C(100,-5)$$

A technician rounds the slopes of $AB$ and $BC$ to the nearest hundredth and concludes that the two sections are equally steep.

![[Slope - Railway Track Survey - Diagram.png]]

**a)** Calculate the slope of each section to the nearest thousandth.

**b)** Determine which section is actually steeper.

**c)** Explain how rounding to the nearest hundredth could have led to an incorrect conclusion.

> [!info]- Hint 1
> For steepness, consider the size of each slope regardless of its sign. Keep extra decimal places until the comparison is complete.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> **(a)** Calculate each slope before rounding.
>
> $$m_{AB}=\frac{-1-(-4)}{-50-(-150)}=\frac{3}{100}=0.030$$
>
> $$m_{BC}=\frac{-5-(-1)}{100-(-50)}=\frac{-4}{150}=-0.026666\ldots\approx -0.027$$
>
> **(b)** The sign indicates whether the track rises or falls from left to right. To compare steepness, compare the magnitudes (the slopes without their signs). The magnitude $0.030$ is greater than $0.026666\ldots$, so **section $AB$ is steeper**.
>
> **(c)** Rounded to the nearest hundredth, the slopes become $0.03$ and $-0.03$. Their rounded magnitudes are identical, hiding the actual difference. Retain exact fractions or additional decimal places until the comparison is complete.
