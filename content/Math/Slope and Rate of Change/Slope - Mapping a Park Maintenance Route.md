# Slope - Mapping a Park Maintenance Route

A park maintenance supervisor uses a coordinate map to plan a route from $A$ to $B$ to $C$ to $D$. Positive $x$-coordinates represent eastward positions; positive $y$-coordinates represent northward positions. Each coordinate unit represents $100$ m.

$$A(-3,-2),\quad B(3,-2),\quad C(3,4),\quad D(-1,7)$$

![[Slope - Mapping a Park Maintenance Route - Diagram.png]]

**a)** Determine the slope of each route segment.

**b)** Identify the segment along which the vehicle travels directly north.

**c)** A worker claims that $CD$ must travel downhill because its slope is negative. Is this conclusion justified? Explain.

> [!info]- Hint 1
> What does each axis measure? Does either one measure elevation?

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> **(a)** Both axes use the same scale of $100$ m per unit, so the scale factor cancels in the slope ratio.
>
> $$m_{AB}=\frac{-2-(-2)}{3-(-3)}=\frac{0}{6}=0$$
>
> For $BC$, the change in $y$ is $4-(-2)=6$ units and the change in $x$ is $3-3=0$. Division by zero is undefined, so the slope is **undefined**.
>
> $$m_{CD}=\frac{7-4}{-1-3}=\frac{3}{-4}=-0.75$$
>
> The negative run for $CD$ is valid because the coordinates are subtracted consistently in the direction of travel.
>
> **(b)** $BC$ travels directly north: the eastward coordinate stays at $3$ while the northward coordinate increases from $-2$ to $4$.
>
> **(c)** The conclusion is not justified. Neither axis measures elevation. A negative slope on this map describes the orientation of the route, not whether the terrain rises or falls.
