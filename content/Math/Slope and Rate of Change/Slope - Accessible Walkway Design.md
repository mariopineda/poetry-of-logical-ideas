# Slope - Accessible Walkway Design

A community centre is constructing an accessible walkway with two inclined sections separated by a horizontal landing. Both coordinates are measured in metres; vertical coordinates are relative to a reference elevation.

$$A(-12,-0.9),\quad B(0,0),\quad C(3,0),\quad D(11,0.8)$$

The designer specifies that neither inclined section may have a slope greater than $0.08$.

![[Slope - Accessible Walkway Design - Diagram.png]]

**a)** Calculate the slope of each section: $AB$, $BC$ and $CD$.

**b)** Identify which inclined sections meet the design requirement.

**c)** Explain the practical purpose of the section with zero slope.

> [!info]- Hint 1
> Compare the two inclined sections individually, not the average slope of the entire walkway.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> **(a)** Use the endpoints of each section. Subtract the vertical coordinates for the signed rise and the horizontal coordinates in the same order for the signed run. Divide rise by run.
>
> $$m_{AB}=\frac{0-(-0.9)}{0-(-12)}=\frac{0.9}{12}=0.075$$
>
> $$m_{BC}=\frac{0-0}{3-0}=\frac{0}{3}=0$$
>
> $$m_{CD}=\frac{0.8-0}{11-3}=\frac{0.8}{8}=0.10$$
>
> **(b)** $AB$ meets the design requirement because $0.075$ is less than the maximum of $0.08$. $CD$ does not meet it because $0.10$ is greater than $0.08$. Compare each inclined section individually; the overall slope could conceal an overly steep section.
>
> **(c)** $BC$ has no change in elevation over a horizontal run of $3$ m. This is a level landing where users can rest between the inclined sections.
