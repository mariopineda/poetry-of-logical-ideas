For the rational function

$$
f(x)=\frac{(x-2)(3x+5)}{(x-2)(x+4)},
$$

determine the coordinates of its removable discontinuity. Also state the vertical asymptote and explain the difference between these two excluded values.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> The original denominator gives the restrictions $x\ne2$ and $x\ne-4$. Cancel $x-2$ **only on the permitted domain**:
>
> $$
> f(x)=\frac{3x+5}{x+4}\qquad(x\ne2,-4).
> $$
>
> The limit at the removed point is
>
> $$
> \lim_{x\to2}f(x)=\frac{3(2)+5}{2+4}=\frac{11}{6}.
> $$
>
> Thus the hole is $\boxed{(2,\tfrac{11}{6})}$. The uncancelled denominator $x+4$ vanishes at $x=-4$; there is a vertical asymptote at $\boxed{x=-4}$.
