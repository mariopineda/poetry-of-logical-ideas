You have accepted a summer job painting your classroom. Before you can start painting, you need to determine the amount of paint required and the cost. Each can holds $3.79$ L of paint and covers $37\text{ m}^2$. The classroom needs two coats of paint.

Work in pairs using metre sticks to measure the dimensions of the classroom. Assume that furniture and fixtures will be removed during painting. Do not stand on desks or chairs.

1. Draw a sketch of each wall to be painted. Label the dimensions in metres, rounding to the nearest tenth of a metre, and show the calculations required to determine the surface area of each wall segment.
2. Determine the total surface area to be painted, in square metres, rounding to the nearest tenth.
3. Determine the amount of paint, rounded to the nearest litre, required for two coats.
4. How many cans of paint, rounded up to the nearest whole can, are required?
5. If each can costs \$38.97, how much will all the paint cost?

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> Answers depend on the measurements of the classroom.
>
> Let the total area of the wall surfaces to be painted for **one coat** be
>
> $$
> A\text{ m}^2.
> $$
>
> If doors, windows, or other surfaces are not being painted, subtract their areas from the wall areas before finding $A$.
>
> ### 1–2. Total wall area
>
> For each rectangular wall segment,
>
> $$
> A_i=(\text{length})(\text{height}).
> $$
>
> Add all wall segments and subtract any openings that are not painted:
>
> $$
> \boxed{A=\sum A_{\text{walls}}-\sum A_{\text{openings}}}.
> $$
>
> ### 3. Paint required
>
> Two coats require coverage of
>
> $$
> 2A\text{ m}^2.
> $$
>
> Since one $3.79$ L can covers $37\text{ m}^2$, the volume of paint required is
>
> $$
> V
> =
> \frac{2A}{37}(3.79).
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \boxed{V\approx0.2049A\text{ L}}.
> $$
>
> Round this value to the nearest litre for the requested report.
>
> ### 4. Number of cans
>
> Each can covers $37\text{ m}^2$, so the number of cans is
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> N=
> \left\lceil
> \frac{2A}{37}
> \right\rceil
> }.
> $$
>
> The ceiling symbol means to round **up** to the next whole can.
>
> ### 5. Total cost
>
> If each can costs \$38.97, then
>
> $$
> \boxed{C=38.97N}.
> $$
>
> Substitute the value of $N$ from part 4 to obtain the total cost.
