Evaluate, if possible.

#### A.

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{4-3n-8n^3}{12n^3+9n^2-20}
$$

#### B.

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^4-5n^3+7}{n(11-n^4)}
$$

#### C.

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(n-5)^4}{(n^2+1)(n-7)}
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A.
>
> $$
> \lim_{n\to\infty}
> \frac{4-3n-8n^3}
> {12n^3+9n^2-20}
> $$
>
> The numerator and denominator have the same degree, so the limit is the ratio of leading coefficients:
>
> $$
> \boxed{-\frac23}.
> $$
>
> ### B.
>
> $$
> \lim_{n\to\infty}
> \frac{n^4-5n^3+7}
> {n(11-n^4)}.
> $$
>
> The denominator has degree $5$ while the numerator has degree $4$, so
>
> $$
> \boxed{0}.
> $$
>
> ### C.
>
> $$
> \lim_{n\to\infty}
> \frac{(n-5)^4}
> {(n^2+1)(n-7)}.
> $$
>
> The numerator has degree $4$ and the denominator has degree $3$.
>
> The expression behaves like
>
> $$
> \frac{n^4}{n^3}=n.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\infty}.
> $$
