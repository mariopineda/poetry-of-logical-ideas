Determine the infinite term for each.

#### A.

$$
7\frac{1}{2}, 7\frac{1}{3}, 7\frac{1}{4}, \ldots
$$

#### B.

$$
\frac{7}{4}, \frac{8}{5}, \frac{9}{6}, \frac{10}{7}, \ldots
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A.
>
> The sequence is
>
> $$
> 7\frac12,\quad
> 7\frac13,\quad
> 7\frac14,\ldots
> $$
>
> The fractional part approaches zero, so
>
> $$
> \boxed{7}
> $$
>
> is the infinite term.
>
> ### B.
>
> The sequence is
>
> $$
> \frac74,\quad
> \frac85,\quad
> \frac96,\quad
> \frac{10}{7},\ldots
> $$
>
> A general term can be written in the form
>
> $$
> \frac{n+6}{n+3}.
> $$
>
> Divide numerator and denominator by $n$:
>
> $$
> \frac{1+\frac6n}{1+\frac3n}.
> $$
>
> As
>
> $$
> n\to\infty,
> $$
>
> this approaches
>
> $$
> \boxed{1}.
> $$
