## Question
*Calculator is not permitted on this question.*

A student is analysing

$$
g(x)=-x^3+4x
$$

and makes these statements:

1. “The graph extends from quadrant II to quadrant IV.”
2. “There is no constant term, so there is no $y$-intercept.”
3. “A cubic function must have exactly two turning points.”
4. “The range is restricted because the leading coefficient is negative.”

A) Identify each statement as **correct or incorrect**. Explain each decision and correct every incorrect statement.

B) Write one possible **cubic function** with a **positive leading coefficient** and a $y$-intercept of $(0,-3)$.

C) Sketch your function from part **B**. Label the $y$-intercept and clearly show its end behaviour.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A. Checking the statements
>
> 1. **Correct.** The function has odd degree and a negative leading coefficient, so its end behaviour is $Q_{\mathrm{II}}\longrightarrow Q_{\mathrm{IV}}$.
>
> 2. **Incorrect.** The constant term is $0$, so $g(0)=0$. There is a $y$-intercept at $(0,0)$.
>
> 3. **Incorrect as a general rule.** A cubic function can have $0$ or $2$ turning points. For example, $y=x^3$ has no turning points. **Marking clarification:** the particular function $g(x)=-x^3+4x$ does have two turning points; the error is claiming that every cubic must have two.
>
> 4. **Incorrect.** A cubic function extends upward without bound at one end and downward without bound at the other. Its range is
>
> $$
> R=\{y\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\}.
> $$
>
> A negative leading coefficient determines the direction of the end behaviour; it does not restrict the range.
>
> ### B. One possible equation
>
> $$
> h(x)=x^3-3.
> $$
>
> The degree is $3$, the leading coefficient is $1>0$, and $h(0)=-3$. Other equations satisfying all three conditions are also valid.
>
> ### C. Sketch of this example
>
> ![[Polynomial Functions 28 Solution Graph.png]]
>
> The curve passes through $(-1,-4)$, $(0,-3)$, and $(1,-2)$. It has no turning points; the horizontal flattening at $(0,-3)$ is not a turning point because the function continues increasing.
>
> $$
> Q_{\mathrm{III}}\longrightarrow Q_{\mathrm{I}}.
> $$
>
> Accept a different correct sketch when it matches the student's valid equation in part **b)**.
