The graph below shows a polynomial function $y=f(x)$. A second function is defined by $g(x)=4f(x)$.

![[diploma-batch04-2014-mc1-polynomial.svg]]

Determine **every intersection point** of the graphs of $f$ and $g$ from the graph. Explain why there are no other intersections, without requiring a formula for $f$.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> At any intersection, the two function values are equal:
>
> $$f(x)=4f(x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -3f(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x)=0.$$
>
> Thus their intersections are **exactly** the $x$-intercepts of the original function. The plotted increasing cubic crosses the $x$-axis only once, at $x=-3$. Consequently the unique intersection is
>
> $$\boxed{(-3,0)}.$$
>
> The function has no other zero, so there are no other intersections; this conclusion does not depend on having an explicit polynomial equation.
