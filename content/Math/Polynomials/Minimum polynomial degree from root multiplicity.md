A polynomial function has a zero at $x=3$ with multiplicity $3$, has a negative leading coefficient, and has an **absolute maximum** over all real values of $x$. Determine the smallest possible degree. Construct one example that has these properties and justify your conclusion.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The polynomial must have even degree and a negative leading coefficient to attain an absolute maximum on $\mathbb R$ (with both ends tending to $-\infty$). A zero of multiplicity exactly $3$ requires degree at least $3$, so the smallest allowable even degree is $\boxed{4}$. A suitable example is
>
> $$\boxed{f(x)=-(x-3)^3(x+1)}.$$
>
> This has a zero of multiplicity exactly $3$ at $x=3$, degree $4$, negative leading coefficient, and tends to $-\infty$ at both ends. Continuity therefore guarantees an absolute maximum.
