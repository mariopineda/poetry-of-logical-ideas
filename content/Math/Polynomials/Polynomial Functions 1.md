State whether or not each of the following functions is a polynomial function? Explain why.

### 1. $f(x) = x^2 + \sqrt x + 17$
### 2. $f(x) = x+\frac{\displaystyle 17}{\displaystyle x}$
### 3. $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x} + 17$
### 4. $f(x) = 18$
### 5. $f(x) = x(x^2+x^{-2}-17)$
### 6. $y=3x$
### 7. $g(x) = 3^x+11$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> A polynomial function may contain only nonnegative integer powers of the variable.
>
> 1.
>
> $$
> f(x)=x^2+\sqrt{x}+17
> =x^2+x^{1/2}+17
> $$
>
> is **not** a polynomial because $\frac12$ is not a nonnegative integer exponent.
>
> 2.
>
> $$
> f(x)=x+\frac{17}{x}
> =x+17x^{-1}
> $$
>
> is **not** a polynomial because of the negative exponent.
>
> 3.
>
> $$
> f(x)=\sqrt[3]{x}+17
> =x^{1/3}+17
> $$
>
> is **not** a polynomial because $\frac13$ is not a nonnegative integer exponent.
>
> 4.
>
> $$
> f(x)=18
> $$
>
> **is** a polynomial: it is a constant polynomial of degree $0$.
>
> 5.
>
> $$
> f(x)=x(x^2+x^{-2}-17)
> =x^3+x^{-1}-17x
> $$
>
> is **not** a polynomial because of the $x^{-1}$ term.
>
> 6.
>
> $$
> y=3x
> $$
>
> **is** a polynomial of degree $1$.
>
> 7.
>
> $$
> g(x)=3^x+11
> $$
>
> is **not** a polynomial because the variable occurs in the exponent.
>
> Therefore, only
>
> $$
> \boxed{4\text{ and }6}
> $$
>
> are polynomial functions.
