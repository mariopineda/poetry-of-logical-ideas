## Question

Consider the polynomial function

$$
f(x)=5x^2-2x^3+7.
$$

1. Write the function in standard form. State its **degree**, **function name**, **leading coefficient**, and **constant term**.

2. Describe its end behaviour using quadrants. Explain how the degree and leading coefficient determine your answer.

3. State the $y$-intercept as an ordered pair, and give the domain and range.

4. A student says, “This function has degree $3$, so it must have exactly $3$ $x$-intercepts.” Explain the error and state the possible numbers of distinct $x$-intercepts for a cubic function.

No calculator is required.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### 1. Standard form and characteristics
>
> $$
> f(x)=-2x^3+5x^2+7.
> $$
>
> - Degree: $3$.
> - Function name: cubic.
> - Leading coefficient: $-2$.
> - Constant term: $7$.
>
> ### 2. End behaviour
>
> $$
> Q_{\mathrm{II}}\longrightarrow Q_{\mathrm{IV}}.
> $$
>
> The odd degree means the ends point in opposite directions. The negative leading coefficient means the graph rises on the left and falls on the right.
>
> $$
> x\to-\infty\implies f(x)\to\infty,\qquad
> x\to\infty\implies f(x)\to-\infty.
> $$
>
> ### 3. Intercept, domain, and range
>
> Since $f(0)=7$, the $y$-intercept is $(0,7)$.
>
> $$
> D=\{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\},\qquad R=\{y\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\}.
> $$
>
> ### 4. Correcting the claim
>
> The degree gives the **maximum**, not the exact number, of distinct $x$-intercepts. A cubic function can have $1$, $2$, or $3$ distinct $x$-intercepts.
