## Question

Consider the function

$$
f(x)=-x^3+3x+2
$$

Graph the function using a graphing calculator. Sketch the graph and determine:

1. The degree and name of the function.
2. The sign of the leading coefficient.
3. The constant term.
4. The $x$-intercept(s) and $y$-intercept.
5. The end behaviour, using quadrant notation.
6. The domain and range.
7. The number of turning points.
8. The coordinates of each turning point, identifying each as a local maximum or local minimum.

Round non-integer coordinates to the nearest hundredth.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> Enter the function into the graphing calculator:
>
> $$
> Y_1=-X^3+3X+2
> $$
>
> A useful viewing window is:
>
> $$
> -4\le X\le4, \qquad -8\le Y\le8
> $$
>
> ![[Polynomial Functions - Analyzing a Cubic Function 1 Graph.png]]
>
> 1. The greatest exponent is $3$. Therefore, the function has **degree 3** and is a **cubic function**.
>
> 2. The leading coefficient is $-1$, so the leading coefficient is **negative**.
>
> 3. The constant term is **$2$**.
>
> 4. The graph has $x$-intercepts at
>
> $$
> (-1,0) \quad \text{and} \quad (2,0).
> $$
>
> At $(-1,0)$, the graph touches the $x$-axis and turns around. The graph has a $y$-intercept at
>
> $$
> (0,2).
> $$
>
> 5. The graph extends from **Quadrant II to Quadrant IV**:
>
> $$
> Q2\rightarrow Q4
> $$
>
> 6. The domain and range are both all real numbers:
>
> $$
> D:\{x\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}
> $$
>
> $$
> R:\{y\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\}
> $$
>
> 7. The graph has **two turning points**.
>
> 8. Use `2nd` `TRACE` and the **minimum** and **maximum** commands to locate the turning points:
>
> - Local minimum: $(-1,0)$
> - Local maximum: $(1,4)$
