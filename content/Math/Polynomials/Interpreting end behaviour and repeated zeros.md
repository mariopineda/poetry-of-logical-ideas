For $P(x)=-3(x-2)^2(x-5)^3$, identify its end behaviour, number of distinct $x$-intercepts, and sign of its $y$-intercept. State whether it can have an absolute maximum on $\mathbb R$, and justify using the degree and leading coefficient.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> In $P(x)=-3(x-2)^2(x-5)^3$, the degree is $2+3=5$ and the leading coefficient is $-3$. An odd-degree polynomial with negative leading coefficient rises to the left and falls to the right:
>
> $$\lim_{x\to-\infty}P(x)=+\infty,\qquad\lim_{x\to+\infty}P(x)=-\infty.$$
>
> The distinct roots are $\boxed{x=2,5}$, with multiplicities $2$ and $3$ respectively. At $x=0$,
>
> $$P(0)=-3(-2)^2(-5)^3=\boxed{1500>0}.$$
>
> Because $P$ is unbounded above as $x\to-\infty$, no absolute maximum exists on $\mathbb R$.
