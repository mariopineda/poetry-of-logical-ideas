Use the chart below to summarize how the sign pf the leading coefficient and degree can be used to determine the end behavior of a polynomial function.

![[Polynomial Functions 4.png .png]]

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> The end behaviour depends on two things:
>
> 1. whether the degree is **even** or **odd**;
> 2. whether the leading coefficient is **positive** or **negative**.
>
> | Degree | Leading coefficient | As $x\to-\infty$ | As $x\to\infty$ | End behaviour |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | even | positive | $f(x)\to\infty$ | $f(x)\to\infty$ | both ends up |
> | even | negative | $f(x)\to-\infty$ | $f(x)\to-\infty$ | both ends down |
> | odd | positive | $f(x)\to-\infty$ | $f(x)\to\infty$ | left down, right up |
> | odd | negative | $f(x)\to\infty$ | $f(x)\to-\infty$ | left up, right down |
>
> A useful summary is:
>
> - **even degree:** both ends point the same way;
> - **odd degree:** the ends point in opposite directions;
> - the **sign of the leading coefficient** determines the direction of the right-hand end.
