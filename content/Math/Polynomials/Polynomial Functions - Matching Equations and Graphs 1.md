## Question

Without using technology, match each polynomial function to **Graph A, B, C, D, E, or F**. Within each equation, the letters represent unspecified real coefficients; the same letter may have a different value in another equation. Therefore, a graphing calculator cannot produce a unique graph for any equation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)&=ax-2,\quad a>0 & g(x)&=ax+3,\quad a<0\\
h(x)&=ax^2+bx+3,\quad a<0 & p(x)&=ax^2+bx-5,\quad a>0\\
q(x)&=ax^3+bx^2+cx,\quad a>0 &
r(x)&=ax^4+bx^3+cx^2+dx+1,\quad a<0
\end{aligned}
$$

![[Polynomial Functions - Matching Equations and Graphs 1 Graphs.png]]

1. Record the six matches.
2. For each function, state its **degree** and whether its leading coefficient is **positive or negative**.
3. Justify the match for $r(x)$ and one other function. For each justification, use at least two features such as shape or turning points, end behaviour, and the $y$-intercept.
4. State the common domain of all six functions in interval notation.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### 1 and 2. Matches and characteristics
>
> | Function | Graph | Degree | Leading coefficient | $y$-intercept | End behaviour |
> |---|---:|---:|---|---:|---|
> | $f(x)=ax-2,\ a>0$ | E | $1$ | positive | $(0,-2)$ | QIII to QI |
> | $g(x)=ax+3,\ a<0$ | A | $1$ | negative | $(0,3)$ | QII to QIV |
> | $h(x)=ax^2+bx+3,\ a<0$ | C | $2$ | negative | $(0,3)$ | QIII to QIV |
> | $p(x)=ax^2+bx-5,\ a>0$ | F | $2$ | positive | $(0,-5)$ | QII to QI |
> | $q(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx,\ a>0$ | B | $3$ | positive | $(0,0)$ | QIII to QI |
> | $r(x)=ax^4+bx^3+cx^2+dx+1,\ a<0$ | D | $4$ | negative | $(0,1)$ | QIII to QIV |
>
> ### 3. Possible justifications
>
> - **$r(x)$ matches Graph D:** It is the only degree-$4$ function. Its negative leading coefficient makes both ends point downward, and its constant term $1$ gives a $y$-intercept of $(0,1)$.
> - **$f(x)$ matches Graph E:** It is linear with a positive leading coefficient, so it rises from left to right. Its constant term $-2$ matches the graph's $y$-intercept.
> - **$g(x)$ matches Graph A:** It is linear with a negative leading coefficient and has a $y$-intercept of $(0,3)$.
> - **$h(x)$ matches Graph C:** It is a downward-opening quadratic with a $y$-intercept of $(0,3)$.
> - **$p(x)$ matches Graph F:** It is an upward-opening quadratic with a $y$-intercept of $(0,-5)$.
> - **$q(x)$ matches Graph B:** It is a positive cubic extending from quadrant III to quadrant I and passes through the origin.
>
> Only the justification for $r(x)$ and one other function is required.
>
> ### 4. Common domain
>
> Every polynomial function has a domain of all real numbers:
>
> $$
> (-\infty,\infty).
> $$
