Find an angle $\alpha$ in Quadrant II and an angle $\beta$ in Quadrant III, each in $[0,2\pi)$, for which

$$
\sec\alpha=\csc\beta=-\sqrt{2}.
$$

State both exact angles in radians and verify both reciprocal ratios.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The reciprocal requirements mean
>
> $$\cos\alpha=\frac{-1}{\sqrt2}=-\frac{\sqrt2}{2},\qquad\sin\beta=\frac{-1}{\sqrt2}=-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}.$$
>
> In Quadrant II the angle with cosine $-\sqrt2/2$ is $\boxed{\alpha=3\pi/4}$. In Quadrant III the angle with sine $-\sqrt2/2$ is $\boxed{\beta=5\pi/4}$. Direct verification gives
>
> $$\sec\frac{3\pi}{4}=\frac{1}{-\sqrt2/2}=-\sqrt2,\qquad\csc\frac{5\pi}{4}=\frac{1}{-\sqrt2/2}=-\sqrt2.$$
>
> Both angles lie in $[0,2\pi)$.
