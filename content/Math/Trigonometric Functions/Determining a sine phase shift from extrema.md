The partial graph of $f(x)=a\sin[b(x-c)]+d$, where $a>0$ and $b>0$, is shown. The horizontal axis uses radians.

![[diploma-batch05-2016-mc20-sine.svg]]

Determine $a$, $b$, $d$, and the **smallest positive phase-shift parameter $c$**. Give $c$ exactly and to the nearest hundredth of a radian. Explain your phase-shift reasoning.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> The maximum height is $2$ and minimum is $-4$, so
>
> $$
> a=\frac{2-(-4)}{2}=3,\qquad
> d=\frac{2+(-4)}{2}=-1.
> $$
>
> The two labelled maxima occur at $x=\frac\pi6$ and $x=\frac{13\pi}6$, a separation of $2\pi$. Thus the period is $2\pi$, so $b=\frac{2\pi}{2\pi}=1$.
>
> For a positive-amplitude sine, a maximum occurs when $x-c=\frac\pi2+2k\pi$. Using $x=\frac\pi6$ gives
>
> $$
> c=\frac\pi6-\frac\pi2-2k\pi
> =-\frac\pi3-2k\pi.
> $$
>
> The smallest **positive** representative is obtained with $k=-1$:
>
> $$
> \boxed{c=\frac{5\pi}{3}\approx 5.24\text{ rad}}.
> $$
>
> A compatible rule is $f(x)=3\sin\left(x-\frac{5\pi}{3}\right)-1$; direct substitution confirms the plotted extrema.
