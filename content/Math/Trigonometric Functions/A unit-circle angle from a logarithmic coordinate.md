A point $P$ on the unit circle has $x$-coordinate $\log_b\sqrt b$, where $b>0$ and $b\ne1$, and has a **negative** $y$-coordinate. Determine the angle $\theta$ in $[0,2\pi)$ and one positive coterminal angle greater than $2\pi$.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The logarithm can be evaluated without choosing a numerical base:
>
> $$\log_b\sqrt b=\log_b b^{1/2}=\frac12,$$
>
> valid for $b>0$, $b\ne1$. A unit-circle point with $x=1/2$ has $\cos\theta=1/2$, and its negative $y$-coordinate puts it in Quadrant IV. The principal angle is
>
> $$\boxed{\theta=\frac{5\pi}{3}}.$$
>
> Add a full revolution to obtain one valid positive coterminal angle greater than $2\pi$:
>
> $$\boxed{\theta+2\pi=\frac{11\pi}{3}}.$$
>
> Both have cosine $1/2$ and negative sine.
