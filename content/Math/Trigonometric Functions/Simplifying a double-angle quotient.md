Simplify $\dfrac{1-\cos(2\theta)}{\sin(2\theta)}$ using double-angle identities. State all values of $\theta$ that are excluded from the domain of the **original** expression.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Use the double-angle identities:
>
> $$1-\cos2\theta=2\sin^2\theta,\qquad\sin2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta.$$
>
> Where the original denominator is nonzero,
>
> $$\frac{1-\cos2\theta}{\sin2\theta}=\frac{2\sin^2\theta}{2\sin\theta\cos\theta}=\boxed{\tan\theta}.$$
>
> The original denominator vanishes when $2\theta=n\pi$, so its actual domain is
>
> $$\boxed{\theta\in\mathbb R\setminus\left\{\frac{n\pi}{2}:n\in\mathbb Z\right\}}.$$
>
> In particular multiples of $\pi$ remain excluded even though the simplified $\tan\theta$ is defined there.
