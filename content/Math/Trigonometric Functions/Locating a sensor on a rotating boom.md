A rotating equipment boom is $80\text{ cm}$ long and sweeps through $120^\circ$. A sensor mounted partway along the boom traces an arc of length $100\text{ cm}$. Determine the distance from the sensor to the **tip** of the boom, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre. Use the accompanying diagram.

![[diploma-2017-new-boom-sensor.svg]]

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Arc length is $s=r\theta$ when the central angle $\theta$ is in radians. Here
>
> $$\theta=120^\circ\left(\frac\pi{180^\circ}\right)=\frac{2\pi}{3}.$$
>
> The sensor is at radius
>
> $$r=\frac{s}{\theta}=\frac{100}{2\pi/3}=\frac{150}{\pi}\approx47.7465\text{ cm}.$$
>
> The whole boom is $80$ cm long, so the **distance from the sensor to the tip** is
>
> $$80-\frac{150}{\pi}\approx32.2535\text{ cm}.$$
>
> To the nearest tenth, the required length is $\boxed{32.3\text{ cm}}$. This is positive and below the full boom length, so the geometry is feasible.
