Determine the **exact** value of

$$
\sin\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)+\cos^2\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right).
$$

Show the special-angle unit-circle values and leave your answer in simplified radical form.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> On the unit circle, $3\pi/4$ is in Quadrant II with reference angle $\pi/4$, and $2\pi/3$ has reference angle $\pi/3$. Therefore
>
> $$\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2},\qquad \cos\frac{2\pi}{3}=-\frac12.$$
>
> Squaring the cosine removes its negative sign:
>
> $$\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}+\cos^2\frac{2\pi}{3}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+\left(-\frac12\right)^2=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+\frac14=\boxed{\frac{2\sqrt2+1}{4}}.$$
>
> This is an exact radical answer, not a decimal approximation.
