Assume $\sin\theta\ne0$ and $\cos\theta\ne0$. Simplify each expression to a constant, then order them **from least to greatest**, explaining the trigonometric identities used:

$$A=\tan^2\theta-\sec^2\theta-1,$$
$$B=\frac{\cot\theta\sin\theta}{\cos\theta},$$
$$C=\frac{\tan\theta}{\sec\theta\sin\theta}-\frac12.$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The Pythagorean identity $\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta=1$ gives
>
> $$A=-(\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta)-1=-1-1=-2.$$
>
> Using $\cot\theta=\cos\theta/\sin\theta$ gives
>
> $$B=\frac{(\cos\theta/\sin\theta)\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=1.$$
>
> Likewise, $\tan\theta=\sin\theta/\cos\theta$ and $\sec\theta=1/\cos\theta$, so
>
> $$C=\frac{(\sin\theta/\cos\theta)}{(1/\cos\theta)\sin\theta}-\frac12=1-\frac12=\frac12.$$
>
> All denominators are nonzero under the given restrictions. Therefore $-2<\frac12<1$ and the order is $\boxed{A<C<B}$.
