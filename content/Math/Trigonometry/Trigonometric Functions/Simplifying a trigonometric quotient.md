Simplify the following expression, preserving the exact restrictions of the original expression:

$$\frac{2\cos^2\theta-\cos(2\theta)}{1+\tan^2\theta}.$$

Use both a double-angle identity and a Pythagorean identity, and state all excluded angles.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Use the double-angle identity $\cos2\theta=2\cos^2\theta-1$. Then the numerator simplifies to
>
> $$2\cos^2\theta-\cos2\theta=2\cos^2\theta-(2\cos^2\theta-1)=1.$$
>
> Next apply the Pythagorean identity $1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta$. On the original domain,
>
> $$\frac{2\cos^2\theta-\cos2\theta}{1+\tan^2\theta}=\frac1{\sec^2\theta}=\boxed{\cos^2\theta}.$$
>
> The original expression requires $\tan\theta$ to be defined; thus $\cos\theta\ne0$, or
>
> $$\boxed{\theta\ne\frac\pi2+n\pi,\quad n\in\mathbb Z}.$$
>
> Do not silently restore excluded inputs after simplification.
