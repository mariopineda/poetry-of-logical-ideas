A line from the origin through $(-3,-2)$ forms angle $\alpha$ in standard position. Another line through $(4,-1)$ forms angle $\beta$. Determine the exact value of $\tan(\alpha+\beta)$ and justify quadrant signs.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The terminal-arm points give
>
> $$\tan\alpha=\frac{-2}{-3}=\frac23,\qquad \tan\beta=\frac{-1}{4}=-\frac14.$$
>
> Angle $\alpha$ lies in quadrant III and $\beta$ in quadrant IV, so these signs are consistent. Use the **tangent sum identity**:
>
> $$\begin{aligned}
> \tan(\alpha+\beta)
> &=\frac{\tan\alpha+\tan\beta}{1-\tan\alpha\tan\beta}\\
> &=\frac{\frac23-\frac14}{1-\left(\frac23\right)\left(-\frac14\right)}\\
> &=\frac{\frac5{12}}{1+\frac16}\\
> &=\frac{\frac5{12}}{\frac76}=\boxed{\frac5{14}}.
> \end{aligned}$$
>
> The denominator is nonzero, so the identity is permissible.
