A terminal arm in standard position intersects the unit circle at $T=\left(\frac35,\frac45\right)$. The angle changes by $-\frac{3\pi}{2}$ radians, as indicated in the schematic.

![[diploma-batch02-unit-circle-initial.svg]]

Determine the exact coordinates of the new terminal-arm intersection. Explain the relationship between this negative rotation and a simpler coterminal rotation.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Adding $2\pi$ to the rotation gives a coterminal change of
>
> $$-\frac{3\pi}{2}+2\pi=\frac\pi2.$$
>
> Thus the final terminal arm is obtained by rotating counterclockwise through $\pi/2$. This sends $(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)$, so
>
> $$\left(\frac35,\frac45\right)\mapsto\boxed{\left(-\frac45,\frac35\right)}.$$
>
> The result is still on the unit circle, because $\left(-\frac45\right)^2+\left(\frac35\right)^2=1$.
