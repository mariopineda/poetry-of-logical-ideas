For the identity

$$\frac{\sin x}{1-\cos x}=\frac{1+\cos x}{\sin x},$$

calculate the **exact value of each side** when $x=\frac\pi3$. Verify that the expressions are defined at that angle.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> At $x=\pi/3$, we have $\sin x=\sqrt3/2$ and $\cos x=1/2$. Consequently,
>
> $$\text{LHS}=\frac{\sqrt3/2}{1-1/2}=\sqrt3,\qquad \text{RHS}=\frac{1+1/2}{\sqrt3/2}=\frac3{\sqrt3}=\sqrt3.$$
>
> Both denominators, $1/2$ and $\sqrt3/2$, are nonzero. Hence
>
> $$\boxed{\text{LHS}=\text{RHS}=\sqrt3}.$$
>
> This is a verification at the specified angle, rather than a proof that the identity holds for every permitted $x$.
