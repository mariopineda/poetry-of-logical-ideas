An angle $\theta$ lies in Quadrant II and satisfies $\tan\theta=-3$. Determine the **exact** value of $\tan(2\theta)$ and show the relevant trigonometric identity.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> Use the tangent double-angle formula:
>
> $$
> \tan(2\theta)=\frac{2\tan\theta}{1-\tan^2\theta}
> =\frac{2(-3)}{1-(-3)^2}
> =\frac{-6}{-8}
> =\boxed{\frac34}.
> $$
>
> The denominator is nonzero, so the tangent value is defined. A Quadrant II tangent is negative, consistent with the given condition.
