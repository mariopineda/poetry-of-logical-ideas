For each situation, determine whether a **combination** or a **permutation** is appropriate. Explain whether order matters.

1. Selecting three photographs from ten to display **together**, without assigning positions.
2. Awarding **first, second, and third prizes** to three of ten photographers.
3. Choosing two volunteers from eight for **identical duties**.
4. Arranging five different books in a row on a shelf.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> 1. **Combination.** The selected group matters, but its internal order does not: $\binom{10}{3}$.
> 2. **Permutation.** First, second, and third prizes are different roles: ${}_{10}P_3$.
> 3. **Combination.** Both volunteers have the same duty, so exchanging them does not change the selection: $\binom82$.
> 4. **Permutation.** Each different ordering of the five distinct books is a different shelf arrangement: $5!$.
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\text{Combinations: 1 and 3; permutations: 2 and 4.}}
> $$
