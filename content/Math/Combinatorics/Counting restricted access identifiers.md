A museum uses three-character entry identifiers. The first character is one of the six letters $A$–$F$. The second character is $X$ or $Y$. The final character must satisfy these rules:

- When the middle character is $X$, the final character is a digit from $1$ through $7$.
- When the middle character is $Y$, the final character is one of $2,4,6,8$.

Calculate the total number of identifiers and show why the two cases must be **added**, not multiplied.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The two middle-character cases cannot occur simultaneously.
>
> With $X$, there are $6\cdot7=42$ identifiers. With $Y$, there are $6\cdot4=24$ identifiers.
>
> Therefore, the total is $\boxed{42+24=66}$ identifiers. Multiplying the case counts would incorrectly require both middle letters in the same code.
