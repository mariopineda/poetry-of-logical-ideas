For each situation, decide whether a **permutation** or a **combination** is the appropriate model. Explain whether changing the order produces a new result.

### A. Choosing four seed varieties from twenty to plant in a garden.
### B. Assigning four different officer positions to eligible volunteers.
### C. Arranging four different miniature sculptures in a row.
### D. Selecting five volunteers for the same cleanup duty from a group of twelve.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A. **Combination.** Only the set of varieties matters; selection order does not.
> ### B. **Permutation.** The distinct officer titles make assignments ordered.
> ### C. **Permutation.** Changing the order changes the sculpture arrangement.
> ### D. **Combination.** Every selected volunteer has the same role, so the order of names does not matter.
>
> **Test:** If swapping two selected items creates a different outcome, count permutations; otherwise, combinations are usually appropriate.
