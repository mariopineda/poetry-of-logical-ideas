Four **different** flags—one bearing a planet, one a comet, one a star, and one a moon—will be placed in a straight row above a science-fair booth.

### A.

How many different orders of the four flags are possible? **Explain your counting strategy** by stating how many choices remain for each position.

### B.

Express the total number of orders using **factorial notation**.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A.
>
> There are \(4\) choices for the first position, then \(3\) for the second, \(2\) for the third, and \(1\) for the last. By the Fundamental Counting Principle,
>
> $$
> 4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1=\boxed{24}.
> $$
>
> Therefore, **24 different flag orders** are possible.
>
> ### B.
>
> The product of the positive integers from \(4\) down to \(1\) is \(4!\):
>
> $$
> \boxed{4!=24}.
> $$
