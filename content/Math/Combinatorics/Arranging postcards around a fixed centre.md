A gallery has $10$ distinct postcards and space for exactly $6$ in one horizontal row. One particular postcard must occupy the **third position**. Determine the number of different displays and justify whether to use permutations or combinations.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Fix the required postcard in position 3. The other five positions must be filled with five different postcards selected and arranged from the remaining nine.
>
> $$ {}_9P_5=9\cdot8\cdot7\cdot6\cdot5=\boxed{15\,120}.$$
>
> Positions matter, so the order of the five chosen postcards matters.
