Evaluate **without using a calculator**. Show the factorial expansions or cancellations that justify each answer.

### A.

$$
11\cdot10\cdot9!
$$

### B.

$$
\frac{13!}{11!}
$$

### C.

$$
\frac{9\cdot7!}{6!}
$$

### D.

$$
\frac{9!}{3!\,6!}
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A.
>
> Because \(11!=11\cdot10\cdot9!\),
>
> $$
> 11\cdot10\cdot9!
> =11!
> =\boxed{39\,916\,800}.
> $$
>
> ### B.
>
> Cancel the common factor \(11!\):
>
> $$
> \frac{13!}{11!}
> =\frac{13\cdot12\cdot11!}{11!}
> =13\cdot12
> =\boxed{156}.
> $$
>
> ### C.
>
> Use \(7!=7\cdot6!\) and cancel:
>
> $$
> \frac{9\cdot7!}{6!}
> =\frac{9\cdot7\cdot6!}{6!}
> =9\cdot7
> =\boxed{63}.
> $$
>
> ### D.
>
> Expand only as far as necessary:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{9!}{3!\,6!}
> &=\frac{9\cdot8\cdot7\cdot6!}{(3\cdot2\cdot1)\,6!}\\
> &=\frac{9\cdot8\cdot7}{6}\\
> &=\frac{504}{6}\\
> &=\boxed{84}.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> The same factorial factor in a numerator and denominator may be cancelled because it is nonzero.
