An exhibition label consists of one word selected from **each column**, in order.

| First word | Second word | Third word |
|---|---|---|
| Northern | Hidden | Forest |
| Coastal | Quiet | River |
| Prairie | Ancient | Sky |
| Alpine | Open | Wildlife |
| Boreal | | |
| Tundra | | |

How many different three-word labels begin with **either Alpine or Boreal**? Show the multiplication principle.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> There are two permissible opening words: **Alpine** and **Boreal**. Either opening word may be paired with any of the four middle words and any of the four final words.
>
> The fundamental counting principle gives
>
> $$
> N=2\times4\times4=\boxed{32}.
> $$
>
> Order matters because the words have distinct roles (first, second, and third), but we do not multiply by $3!$ because those positions are predetermined.
