In each situation, **all items or people are distinct**, and every item or person must be used exactly once. Write a factorial expression and evaluate it.

### A.

A planetarium has **eight different photographs** to arrange from left to right on a display wall. How many arrangements are possible?

### B.

Six volunteers will each take **one of six different jobs** at a science festival. How many assignments are possible?

### C.

A short-film club will screen **five different documentaries**, one per evening for five evenings. How many screening schedules are possible?

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A.
>
> The eight photographs can fill the eight positions in \(8,7,\ldots,1\) ways:
>
> $$
> 8!=8\cdot7\cdot6\cdot5\cdot4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1
> =\boxed{40\,320}.
> $$
>
> ### B.
>
> Each volunteer receives one different job. Assigning the jobs in order gives \(6\) choices, then \(5\), and so on:
>
> $$
> 6!=6\cdot5\cdot4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1
> =\boxed{720}.
> $$
>
> ### C.
>
> The first evening can feature any of the \(5\) films. There are then \(4\) choices for the second evening, and so on:
>
> $$
> 5!=5\cdot4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1
> =\boxed{120}.
> $$
>
> In all three cases, the objects are distinct, their **order or assignment matters**, and none can be reused.
