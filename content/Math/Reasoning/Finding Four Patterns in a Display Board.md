An exhibit uses four digital indicators. The numbers displayed by each indicator follow their own rule. **Assume each rule is either arithmetic (a constant difference) or geometric (a constant ratio).** The first three observations are shown.

| Indicator | Observation 1 | Observation 2 | Observation 3 | Observation 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| North | 3 | 6 | 9 | $A$ |
| East | 128 | 64 | 32 | $B$ |
| South | 7 | 11 | 15 | $C$ |
| West | 2 | 6 | 18 | $D$ |

Determine $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$ and explain each pattern. Present the final values in **North, East, South, West** order.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> - **North:** The constant difference is $+3$, so $A=9+3=12$.
> - **East:** The constant ratio is $\tfrac12$, so $B=32\left(\tfrac12\right)=16$.
> - **South:** The constant difference is $+4$, so $C=15+4=19$.
> - **West:** The constant ratio is $3$, so $D=18(3)=54$.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{(A,B,C,D)=(12,16,19,54)}.
> $$
>
> The arithmetic-or-geometric assumption is necessary: without restricting the permitted pattern types, any finite sequence could have multiple possible continuations.
