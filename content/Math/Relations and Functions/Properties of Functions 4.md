If $f(x) = \frac{3x+4}{6-x}$ and $g(x)=\frac{x-1}{x+2}$, then determine $f \circ g(x)$. Simplify your answer.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> We need
>
> $$
> (f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x)).
> $$
>
> Given
>
> $$
> f(x)=\frac{3x+4}{6-x}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> g(x)=\frac{x-1}{x+2},
> $$
>
> substitute $g(x)$ into $f$:
>
> $$
> f(g(x))
> =
> \frac{
> 3\left(\frac{x-1}{x+2}\right)+4
> }{
> 6-\left(\frac{x-1}{x+2}\right)
> }.
> $$
>
> Simplify the numerator:
>
> $$
> 3\left(\frac{x-1}{x+2}\right)+4
> =
> \frac{7x+5}{x+2}.
> $$
>
> Simplify the denominator:
>
> $$
> 6-\frac{x-1}{x+2}
> =
> \frac{5x+13}{x+2}.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> (f\circ g)(x)
> =
> \frac{7x+5}{5x+13}
> }.
> $$
>
> The composition requires
>
> $$
> x\neq-2
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> g(x)\neq6.
> $$
>
> Solving
>
> $$
> \frac{x-1}{x+2}=6
> $$
>
> gives
>
> $$
> x=-\frac{13}{5}.
> $$
>
> Thus the restrictions are
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> x\neq-2,\ -\frac{13}{5}
> }.
> $$
