The graph of $y=f(x)$ crosses the $x$-axis at the three labelled points $A$, $B$, and $C$.

![[diploma-batch05-2016-mc2-invariants.svg]]

Of the following transformations, determine which **always leaves all three labelled points fixed**, and explain why: a nonzero vertical scale $y=af(x)$, a horizontal scale $y=f(2x)$, an upward translation $y=f(x)+2$, or a rightward translation $y=f(x-2)$.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> The three points are zeros of $f$: $(-4,0)$, $(-1,0)$ and $(3,0)$. For any nonzero $a$, $af(x)=0$ whenever $f(x)=0$, and the $x$-coordinates do not change. Thus each labelled point is invariant under $\boxed{y=af(x),\ a\ne0}$.
>
> A horizontal scaling changes the $x$-coordinates to $-2$, $-\frac12$ and $\frac32$. An upward shift changes each $y$-coordinate from $0$ to $2$; a rightward shift changes each $x$-coordinate by $2$. Those transformations therefore do not fix all three labelled points.
