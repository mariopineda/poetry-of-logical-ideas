Draw a rough sketch of the following functions. Identify vertex, intercepts and asymptotes.

1.

$$
y=-|x+2|-1
$$
2.

$$
y=\frac{1}{x-2}+3
$$
3.

$$
y=(x-2)^2-2
$$
4.

$$
y=-\sqrt{x-4}
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### 1.
>
> $$
> y=-|x+2|-1
> $$
>
> - vertex: $\boxed{(-2,-1)}$
> - axis of symmetry: $\boxed{x=-2}$
> - $y$-intercept: $\boxed{(0,-3)}$
> - no $x$-intercepts, since $y\leq-1$
> - no asymptotes
>
> The graph is an upside-down $V$.
>
> ### 2.
>
> $$
> y=\frac{1}{x-2}+3
> $$
>
> is a reciprocal graph shifted right $2$ and up $3$.
>
> - vertical asymptote: $\boxed{x=2}$
> - horizontal asymptote: $\boxed{y=3}$
>
> For the $x$-intercept,
>
> $$
> 0=\frac1{x-2}+3
> \Rightarrow
> x=\frac53.
> $$
>
> Thus the $x$-intercept is
>
> $$
> \boxed{\left(\frac53,0\right)}.
> $$
>
> The $y$-intercept is
>
> $$
> y(0)=-\frac12+3=\frac52,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \boxed{\left(0,\frac52\right)}.
> $$
>
> ### 3.
>
> $$
> y=(x-2)^2-2
> $$
>
> - vertex: $\boxed{(2,-2)}$
> - axis: $\boxed{x=2}$
> - $x$-intercepts:
>
> $$
> (x-2)^2=2
> \Rightarrow
> x=2\pm\sqrt2
> $$
>
> - $y$-intercept: $\boxed{(0,2)}$
> - no asymptotes
>
> ### 4.
>
> $$
> y=-\sqrt{x-4}
> $$
>
> - endpoint: $\boxed{(4,0)}$
> - $x$-intercept: $\boxed{(4,0)}$
> - no $y$-intercept
> - domain: $\boxed{[4,\infty)}$
> - range: $\boxed{(-\infty,0]}$
> - no asymptotes
>
> The graph extends rightward and downward from $(4,0)$.
