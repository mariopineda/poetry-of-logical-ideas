The graph of $y=\sqrt{f(x)}$ is shown, where $f$ is a quadratic function.

![[diploma-batch05-2016-mc12-sqrt-graph.svg]]

Determine the maximum possible output of $f$. Decide, with justification, whether the point $(3,55)$ could lie on the graph of $y=f(x)$. As an additional check, derive a quadratic rule consistent with the three labelled points of the square-root graph.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> The graphed square-root outputs are between $0$ and $7$. Since $\sqrt{f(x)}\le7$ wherever defined, $f(x)\le 7^2=49$ on that domain. A downward-opening quadratic with this maximum cannot reach $55$, so $\boxed{(3,55)\text{ is impossible}}$.
>
> The endpoints of the square-root graph correspond to zeros $f(-6)=f(8)=0$. The axis of symmetry is halfway between them, $x=1$. The maximum is $f(1)=49$, hence
>
> $$
> f(x)=49-(x-1)^2.
> $$
>
> Indeed $f(3)=49-4=45\ne55$, independently confirming the decision. The original quadratic has domain $\mathbb R$ and range $(-\infty,49]$.
