An engineer is considering the cubic function $P(x)=(x-2)(x-5)(x+3)$.

The graph of $P$ is shown, without intercept labels:

![[batch09-cubic-intercepts.svg]]

From the **graph**, estimate the $x$-intercepts and determine the left- and right-end behaviour. Then verify the intercepts using the equation. Explain why this graph has **two positive roots, one negative root, and a positive leading coefficient**.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> The curve crosses the horizontal axis at approximately $-3$, $2$, and $5$. Factoring confirms
>
> $$P(x)=0\iff x=-3,\ 2,\ 5.$$
>
> Therefore the intercepts are $\boxed{(-3,0),(2,0),(5,0)}$. The first two factors have positive zeros $2$ and $5$; $x+3$ has the negative zero $-3$.
>
> The leading term is $+x^3$. Consequently
>
> $$\boxed{P(x)\to-\infty\text{ as }x\to-\infty},\qquad \boxed{P(x)\to+\infty\text{ as }x\to+\infty}.$$
>
> This agrees with the graph descending on the far left and rising on the far right.
