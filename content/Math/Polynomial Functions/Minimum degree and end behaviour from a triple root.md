A polynomial $P$ has a zero at $x=-1$ of multiplicity $3$, satisfies $P(0)=-6$, and has an **absolute** minimum value of $-54$. Determine the least possible degree and the necessary sign of the leading coefficient at that least degree. Construct a polynomial verifying that your answer is possible.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> An odd-multiplicity factor $(x+1)^3$ alone gives a cubic, which cannot have an absolute minimum over all real $x$. The least possible degree is at least $4$, requiring a positive leading coefficient for an absolute minimum. The example $P(x)=2(x+1)^3(x-3)$ satisfies $P(0)=-6$. Its derivative is $P'(x)=8(x+1)^2(x-2)$, so it decreases until $x=2$ and increases afterward; $P(2)=2(27)(-1)=-54$. Thus $\boxed{\text{degree }4,\text{ positive leading coefficient}}$.
