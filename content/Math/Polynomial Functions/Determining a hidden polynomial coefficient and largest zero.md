The polynomial $P(x)=x^5-2x^4-9x^3+2x^2+kx$ has zeros at $x=-1$ and $x=-2$. Determine $k$, factor the resulting polynomial completely, and give its greatest real zero.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Substitute the known zero $x=-1$ into the original polynomial:
>
> $$0=P(-1)=-1-2+9+2-k=8-k,$$
>
> so $\boxed{k=8}$. This also satisfies the second known zero, since $P(-2)=0$ after substitution.
>
> Factoring independently gives
>
> $$P(x)=x\bigl(x^4-2x^3-9x^2+2x+8\bigr)=\boxed{x(x+2)(x+1)(x-1)(x-4)}.$$
>
> Multiplying the factors reproduces the original coefficients. The real zeros are $-2,-1,0,1,4$, so the **greatest** is $\boxed{x=4}$.
