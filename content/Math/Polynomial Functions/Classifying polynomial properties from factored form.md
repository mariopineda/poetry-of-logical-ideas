For $y=(x-2)^2(x+4)$, state the degree, real $x$-intercepts, domain, and range. Explain how multiplicities influence the intercepts.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> For $y=(x-2)^2(x+4)$, the total degree is $2+1=\boxed{3}$, with positive leading coefficient.
>
> The $x$-intercepts satisfy $y=0$: $x=2$ is a root of multiplicity $2$ (the graph touches the axis there), and $x=-4$ is a simple root (the graph crosses the axis there).
>
> Because this polynomial is defined for every real input, its **domain** is $\boxed{\mathbb R}$. Because it is an odd-degree polynomial with positive leading coefficient, its end behaviour is $y\to-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$ and $y\to+\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$. Continuity then gives **range** $\boxed{\mathbb R}$.
