The height of a released weather balloon's sensor above the ground is approximated during its fall by

$$h(t)=84-4.9t^2,$$

where $h$ is measured in metres and $t\ge0$ in seconds. How long after release will it be at a height of $14$ metres? Give your answer to the nearest tenth of a second and state why only one algebraic root makes sense.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
> Set $h(t)=14$ rather than $0$:
>
> $$84-4.9t^2=14\quad\Longrightarrow\quad4.9t^2=70$$
> $$t=\pm\sqrt{\frac{70}{4.9}}\approx\pm3.7796.$$
>
> Because $t\ge0$, the physical answer is $\boxed{t\approx3.8\text{ s}}$. The negative root would represent a time before the model's starting event.
