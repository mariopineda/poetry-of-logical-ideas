## Question

Use exponent laws to simplify the following:

1.

$$
\left( a^2 \right)^{\frac{3}{2}}
$$
2.

$$
\left( 16x^2 \right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
$$
3.

$$
\left( 2x^{\frac{1}{3}} \right) \left( -4x^{\frac{5}{3}} \right)
$$
4.

$$
\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}
$$
5.

$$
\left( \frac{16x^8}{y^{-2}} \right)^{\frac{3}{4}}
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> Using exponent laws:
>
> 1.
>
> $$
> (a^2)^{3/2}=|a|^3.
> $$
>
> If the course convention assumes $a\geq0$, this is simply
>
> $$
> \boxed{a^3}.
> $$
>
> 2.
>
> $$
> (16x^2)^{1/2}=4|x|.
> $$
>
> If $x\geq0$, this becomes
>
> $$
> \boxed{4x}.
> $$
>
> 3.
>
> $$
> \left(2x^{1/3}\right)\left(-4x^{5/3}\right)
> =-8x^{6/3}
> =\boxed{-8x^2}.
> $$
>
> 4.
>
> $$
> \frac{x^{1/2}}{x^{1/2}}
> =x^0
> =\boxed{1},
> \qquad x>0.
> $$
>
> 5.
>
> $$
> \left(\frac{16x^8}{y^{-2}}\right)^{3/4}
> =
> \left(16x^8y^2\right)^{3/4}.
> $$
>
> For positive variables,
>
> $$
> 16^{3/4}x^6y^{3/2}
> =
> \boxed{8x^6y^{3/2}}.
> $$
