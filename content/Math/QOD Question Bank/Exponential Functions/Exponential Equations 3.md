## Question

A country's population is increasing at an annual rate of 2.2%. The current population is approximatelly 14 million. The growth in population can be modelled by the exponential function $P=P_0(1.022)^t$, $t$ is the number of years since the population was $P_0$. Assuming the population growth continues at the same rate,

1. determine, to the nearest million, the approximate population 6 years from now
2. determine the number of complete years required for the population to double.
3. Suggest an appropriate domain and range within the context of this problem.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> The model is
>
> $$
> P=14(1.022)^t,
> $$
>
> where $P$ is measured in millions.
>
> ### 1.
>
> After $6$ years,
>
> $$
> P
> =
> 14(1.022)^6
> \approx15.95.
> $$
>
> To the nearest million,
>
> $$
> \boxed{16\text{ million}}.
> $$
>
> ### 2.
>
> For the population to double,
>
> $$
> 28=14(1.022)^t.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> 2=(1.022)^t.
> $$
>
> Taking logarithms,
>
> $$
> t
> =
> \frac{\log2}{\log1.022}
> \approx31.85.
> $$
>
> Therefore, the population first reaches at least double its current size after
>
> $$
> \boxed{32\text{ complete years}}.
> $$
>
> ### 3.
>
> A suitable continuous-model domain is
>
> $$
> \boxed{t\in[0,\infty)}
> $$
>
> and the corresponding range is
>
> $$
> \boxed{P\in[14,\infty)}
> $$
>
> with $P$ measured in millions.
>
> If the model is evaluated only at complete years, the domain can instead be taken as
>
> $$
> \{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}.
> $$
