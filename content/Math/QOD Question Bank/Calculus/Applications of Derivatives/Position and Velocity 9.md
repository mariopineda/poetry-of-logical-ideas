## Question

The position of an object is

$$
s(t)=t^3-6t^2,
\qquad
t\geq0,
$$

where $s$ is measured in kilometres, $t$ is measured in hours, North is positive, and South is negative.

When is the object moving North and toward the origin?

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> Differentiate:
>
> $$
> v(t)=3t^2-12t=3t(t-4).
> $$
>
> For $t\geq0$, the object moves North when
>
> $$
> v(t)>0,
> $$
>
> which occurs for
>
> $$
> t>4.
> $$
>
> The position is
>
> $$
> s(t)=t^2(t-6).
> $$
>
> For $t>0$, the object is South of the origin when
>
> $$
> s(t)<0,
> $$
>
> which occurs for
>
> $$
> 0<t<6.
> $$
>
> To move North **toward** the origin, the object must be South of the origin while moving North.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{4<t<6}.
> $$
