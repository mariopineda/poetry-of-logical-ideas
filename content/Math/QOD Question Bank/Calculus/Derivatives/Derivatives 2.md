## Question

For each function, sketch the corresponding derivative function.

### A.

$$
f(x)=x^2-4x+3
$$

### B.

$$
g(x)=x^3-3x
$$

### C.

$$
h(x)=|x|
$$

For each derivative sketch, identify any zeros of the derivative and any point where the derivative does not exist.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
>
> ### A.
>
> For
>
> $$
> f(x)=x^2-4x+3,
> $$
>
> differentiate:
>
> $$
> f'(x)=2x-4.
> $$
>
> So the derivative is the straight line
>
> $$
> \boxed{y=2x-4}.
> $$
>
> It crosses the $x$-axis when
>
> $$
> 2x-4=0,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \boxed{x=2}.
> $$
>
> ### B.
>
> For
>
> $$
> g(x)=x^3-3x,
> $$
>
> differentiate:
>
> $$
> g'(x)=3x^2-3
> =
> 3(x-1)(x+1).
> $$
>
> Thus the derivative is an upward-opening parabola with zeros at
>
> $$
> \boxed{x=-1,\ 1}.
> $$
>
> Its vertex is
>
> $$
> \boxed{(0,-3)}.
> $$
>
> ### C.
>
> For
>
> $$
> h(x)=|x|,
> $$
>
> the slope is constant on each side of the origin:
>
> $$
> h'(x)=
> \begin{cases}
> -1, & x<0,\\
> 1, & x>0.
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> At
>
> $$
> x=0,
> $$
>
> the graph of $h$ has a sharp corner, so the derivative does not exist there.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> h'(x)=
> \begin{cases}
> -1, & x<0,\\
> 1, & x>0,
> \end{cases}
> \qquad
> h'(0)\text{ does not exist}
> }.
> $$
