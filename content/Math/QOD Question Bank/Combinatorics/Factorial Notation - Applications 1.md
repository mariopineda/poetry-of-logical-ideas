## Question

### A.

Manually calculate:

$$
\frac{9!}{6!}
$$

### B.

Eight students are arranging themselves in a line for a photograph. One particular student must stand in the first position.

How many different arrangements are possible?

### C.

Seven runners compete in a race.

How many different arrangements are possible for first, second, and third place?

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A.
>
> $$
> \frac{9!}{6!}
> =
> \frac{9(8)(7)(6!)}{6!}
> =
> 9(8)(7)
> =
> 504
> $$
>
> ### B.
>
> One position is fixed, leaving \(7\) students to arrange.
>
> $$
> 7!=5040
> $$
>
> ### C.
>
> $$
> 7(6)(5)=210
> $$
>
> Equivalently,
>
> $$
> \frac{7!}{4!}=210
> $$
