Find $y'$.

### A.

$$
xy+x^3=y^2-4
$$

### B.

$$
y-x^2y^3=x+y^2
$$

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> ### A.
>
> Differentiate:
>
> $$
> y+xy'+3x^2=2yy'.
> $$
>
> Collect the $y'$ terms:
>
> $$
> (2y-x)y'=y+3x^2.
> $$
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> y'=
> \frac{y+3x^2}{2y-x}
> }.
> $$
>
> ### B.
>
> Differentiate:
>
> $$
> y'
> -
> \left(2xy^3+3x^2y^2y'\right)
> =
> 1+2yy'.
> $$
>
> Collect the $y'$ terms:
>
> $$
> y'(1-3x^2y^2-2y)
> =
> 1+2xy^3.
> $$
>
> Thus,
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> y'
> =
> \frac{1+2xy^3}
> {1-3x^2y^2-2y}
> }.
> $$
