Let

$$
f(x)=x^3-3x^2.
$$

Without graphing technology, sketch the derivative function $y=f'(x)$.

As part of your sketch, identify:

1. the zeros of $f'(x)$;
2. where $f'(x)>0$;
3. where $f'(x)<0$.

## Solution

> [!example]- Show solution
>
> Differentiate:
>
> $$
> f'(x)=3x^2-6x
> =
> 3x(x-2).
> $$
>
> The derivative is an upward-opening parabola.
>
> ### Zeros
>
> $$
> 3x(x-2)=0
> $$
>
> gives
>
> $$
> \boxed{x=0,\ 2}.
> $$
>
> ### Sign
>
> - for $x<0$, both factors are negative, so $f'(x)>0$;
> - for $0<x<2$, the factors have opposite signs, so $f'(x)<0$;
> - for $x>2$, both factors are positive, so $f'(x)>0$.
>
> Therefore,
>
> $$
> \boxed{f'(x)>0\text{ on }(-\infty,0)\cup(2,\infty)}
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \boxed{f'(x)<0\text{ on }(0,2)}.
> $$
>
> A correct sketch is the parabola
>
> $$
> \boxed{y=3x(x-2)}
> $$
>
> crossing the $x$-axis at $0$ and $2$.
