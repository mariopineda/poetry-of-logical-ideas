## Why does my unit exam have two parts?

Unit exams are split across **two consecutive classes** so that every student has approximately **twice the normal class time** to complete the exam.

Part A is normally **written response**. Part B is normally **multiple choice and numerical response**.

Together, Part A and Part B make up **one unit exam**.

This extended exam format is also why **extra exam time is not an available accommodation**.

## How is my Part A and Part B exam mark calculated?

The points you earn on Part A and Part B are added together.

Your percentage is then calculated from the **total possible points for Part A and Part B combined**.

## What happens if I miss Part A or Part B?

The part you miss receives a mark of **zero**.

The points you earn on the part you do write still count, and your exam percentage is calculated from the total possible points for **both** Part A and Part B.

Missing one part will almost certainly result in a very low overall exam mark, so it is strongly discouraged.

You also **cannot make up only the part you missed**. For example, if you miss Part A, you cannot write Part A when you come to write Part B the next class.

## Can I write only the part I missed later?

No. There is **no late write for only Part A or only Part B**.

You may request a late write using the normal late-write procedure. If approved, you must write a **new, complete exam covering the entire unit**, including the material assessed in both Part A and Part B.

The late write is completed as **one exam**, rather than being split over two classes.

## What happens if I miss both Part A and Part B?

Your mark for the unit exam will be **zero**.

You may request a late write by following the normal late-write procedure.

Alternatively, if you meet all the eligibility requirements, you may later select that unit for a replacement exam.
