## What counts as cheating?

If you present work as your own when it is **not actually your own work**, that is cheating.

The work might come from:

* another student;
* a website;
* ChatGPT or another AI tool; or
* another source.

This applies to exams, QODs, and classwork.

## Can I work with another student?

Yes, during regular classwork and QODs.

Real collaboration means:

* both students contribute;
* both students understand the work; and
* both students can explain what they did.

If another student does the work and you simply copy it, that is **not collaboration**. It is cheating.

Students may not collaborate during exams.

## Can I use AI to help me learn?

Yes, AI may be used as a learning tool during regular classwork.

For example, AI may help explain a concept or help you understand how to approach a problem.

However, if AI does the work for you and you submit that work as your own, that is cheating.

The goal is for **you to learn the mathematics**. AI is useful when it supports your learning, not when it replaces it.

## What happens if I cheat?

The work receives a mark of **zero**.

For a first incident:

* the incident will be recorded in your student profile; and
* you will be referred to the department head.

If academic dishonesty happens again, you will be referred to your assistant principal.

Further consequences may include additional academic disciplinary action, suspension, or removal from the course.

## What if I access a phone, smartwatch, smart glasses, notes, or another unauthorized item during an exam?

It will be treated as **cheating**.

It is not necessary to prove that you used the item to find an answer. Accessing an unauthorized device or material during an exam is enough.

## What if I communicate with another student during an exam?

It will be treated as **cheating**.

Students may not communicate with one another during an exam.
